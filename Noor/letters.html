Short vs Long Vowels

Diacritics (tashkeel) show the short vowel sounds that sit above or below a letter.

Fatḥa ـَ

A short "a" sound, written as a small diagonal stroke above the letter.

بَ = ba

Kasra ـِ

A short "i" sound, written as a small diagonal stroke below the letter.

بِ = bi

Ḍamma ـُ

A short "u" sound, written as a tiny loop above the letter.

بُ = bu

Sukun ـْ

Marks "no vowel" — the letter is pronounced with no short vowel after it.

بْ = b

Shadda ـّ

Doubles the letter's sound, as if writing it twice.

بَّ = bba

Long vowels stretch the sound using the letters ا، و، ي — for example با (baa) is longer than بَ (ba).

How Letters Join

Most Arabic letters change shape slightly depending on where they sit in a word.

LetterInitial (start)Medial (middle)Final (end)
ببــبــب
ممــمــم
سســســس
ننــنــن

A few letters — like ا، د، ذ، ر، ز، و — never connect to the letter that follows them, even in the middle of a word.
